Chapter 12 Group Lab: Chatbot Design with Google AI Studio 10 pts

CAI1001C: Artificial Intelligence (AI) Thinking · Miami Dade College
What you're doing: In the GP demo, you built a rule-based chatbot by coding keywords and responses in Python. Now you'll try a completely different approach — using Google AI Studio to build a chatbot by writing a system prompt in plain English. Instead of if/elif logic, you'll give the AI natural language instructions about who it should be, what it should know, and how it should behave.

What you need: A laptop or phone with a browser. Go to aistudio.google.com and sign in with a Google account. Select any available Gemini model and open a new chat.

Submission: Complete this worksheet as a group. Type your answers in the fields below, then Print → Save as PDF and submit one copy per group via Canvas.

Part 2: Write Your System Prompt (5 minutes)

A system prompt tells the AI how to behave — its role, knowledge, tone, and limitations. In Google AI Studio, paste your system prompt into the "System instructions" field at the top of the chat.

Task 2.1: Write Your System Prompt

Write a system prompt for your business chatbot. Include: (1) who the chatbot is, (2) what it knows, (3) its tone/personality, and (4) what it should do when it doesn't know something. Copy this into Google AI Studio's System Instructions field.

💡 Connection to the GP: In your Python chatbot, you defined intents with keywords and responses in a dictionary. A system prompt does the same thing — but in natural language instead of code. The AI figures out the intent matching on its own.

Part 5: Reflection

Task 5.1: System Prompt vs. Python Code

In the GP, you built a chatbot with keywords, dictionaries, and if/elif logic. In this lab, you built one by writing a paragraph of instructions. Compare the two approaches in 2–3 sentences.

Task 5.2: Did Your Chatbot Disclose It Was AI?

During your testing, did the chatbot ever tell the user it was an AI — without being asked? Did it claim to be human? Or was it ambiguous?

Wrap-Up Discussion: Your instructor will ask:
  1. Did anyone's system prompt cause the chatbot to hallucinate — make up information that wasn't in the prompt?
  2. How did edge case handling compare between the Python chatbot (GP) and the AI Studio chatbot?
  3. Should chatbots always disclose they're AI? What did your group observe?
