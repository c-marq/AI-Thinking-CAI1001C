Non-Guided Project — Chapters 1 & 2 — Privacy, Power, and Informed Consent
Individual Work · 30 points · Due Sunday May 17, 11:59 PM EST
💡 WHAT THIS ASSIGNMENT IS ABOUT In class, your group used a no-code AI tool (Google AI Studio) and evaluated a public IoT dataset. You experienced AI from both sides: as a user, and as a data analyst looking in.

Now go deeper, on your own. Pick one real AI system you use regularly — not one we used in class — and audit it. You’re going to figure out what it does, what data it collects about you, what it can infer that you never told it, and whether you really agreed to all of it.

No coding. This is a thinking and investigating assignment.
⚠️ HOW TO PICK YOUR SYSTEM Good candidates: Spotify, Netflix, TikTok, Instagram, Snapchat, YouTube, Google Maps, Waze, Uber/Lyft, Amazon, Apple Photos, Google Photos, Siri, Alexa, Gmail (smart compose), Grammarly, Duolingo, a fitness tracker app, a smart home device, a banking app’s fraud alerts.

Don’t pick Google AI Studio — we already used it in class. Pick something that’s part of your real daily life.
Section 1 — Pick Your System 5 pts

1.1   In two or three sentences, what does this system do for you? Skip any jargon — describe what it actually accomplishes in your life.

Section 2 — Application Category (Chapter 1 Framework) 5 pts
💡 REMINDER — THE FIVE CATEGORIES Recognition (identifies patterns) · Prediction (forecasts what’s likely) · Generation (creates new content) · Recommendation (suggests what you’d like) · Optimization (finds best solution among many)

2.1   Which category best fits the system you chose? (A system can fit more than one — name your primary, and any others that apply.)

2.2   Justify your classification with two specific examples of things this system has actually done for you. Be concrete.

Take at least one screenshot of where you found this information — the settings screen or the relevant section of the privacy policy. This is your evidence.

Section 4 — What Can It Learn That You Didn’t Tell It? 5 pts
⚠️ THINK BACK TO MARIA’S THERMOSTAT Maria never told her thermostat her sleep schedule, when she went to work, or that her family was at a quinceañera last Thursday. But the thermostat figured all of that out from temperature readings and motion data alone. Your AI system probably does the same kind of inference.

4.1   List three things your chosen AI system probably knows about you that you never explicitly told it. Be specific — not just “my preferences” but what preferences and how it could know them.

Section 5 — Ethics Stress Test 5 pts
🛑 THREE HARD QUESTIONS Most people never ask these. You’re going to answer all three.

5.1   Consent.   When you first signed up, did you actually read the terms / privacy policy? If yes, what stood out? If no, why not?

5.2   Third-party access.   Based on what you found in Section 3, who else might have access to your data from this system? (advertisers, partner companies, government requests, law enforcement, anyone else)

5.3   Breach scenario.   If this company had a data breach tomorrow and everything they have on you was published online, what’s the worst thing that could happen to you specifically? (Be honest — this varies a lot by what the system collects.)

Section 6 — Closing Reflection 2 pts
💡 THINK ABOUT IT Now that you’ve audited this system, you know more about it than 99% of its users. So what?

6.1   Will you change anything about how you use this system after this audit? Why or why not? Be honest — “no, the value still outweighs the privacy cost for me” is a valid answer if you can defend it.

6.2   In one or two sentences: what would real informed consent look like for this product? What would the company have to do for you to feel like you genuinely understood and agreed to its data practices?
