## Overview

In this activity, we'll apply the AI Readiness Framework from Chapter 13 to assess a real company's preparedness for AI. You'll practice scoring each of the five readiness dimensions, justifying your scores with evidence, and identifying actionable recommendations.

This is a class walkthrough — follow along, participate in the discussion, and keep this guide as a reference for the Group Lab and Homework.

---

## Learning Objectives

By completing this activity, you will be able to:

- Apply the AI Readiness Framework to a real organization
- Score each of the 5 dimensions using the 1–5 scale with evidence-based justification
- Identify an organization's biggest AI readiness gaps
- Recommend specific, actionable next steps for improvement

---

## The AI Readiness Framework — Quick Reference

| Dimension | What It Assesses | Key Question |
|---|---|---|
| 🗃️ **Data Infrastructure** | Data collection, quality, organization, and accessibility | Is the right data being collected, and can the organization access it when needed? |
| 👥 **Talent & Skills** | AI expertise, data skills, and training plans | Does the team have the skills to build, maintain, and interpret AI systems? |
| 💻 **Computing Resources** | Hardware, cloud access, GPU availability | Can the infrastructure handle AI workloads at the needed scale? |
| ⚖️ **Ethical Preparedness** | Bias checks, fairness processes, accountability | Are there processes to catch bias, protect privacy, and ensure fairness? |
| 🏢 **Organizational Culture** | Leadership support, workforce readiness, willingness to adopt | Does the organization support AI adoption from the top down? |

### Scoring Guide (Use for All Dimensions)

| Score | Level | What It Means |
|:---:|---|---|
| 1 | Not Ready | No capability or awareness in this area |
| 2 | Early Stage | Some basics exist but not organized or systematic |
| 3 | Developing | Systematic efforts underway but gaps remain |
| 4 | Strong | Solid capability with clear processes and roles |
| 5 | Advanced | Best-in-class; continuously improving and well-integrated |

## Class Walkthrough: Scoring a Real Company

### Step 1: Choose the Company

**Instructor note:** Pick one company the entire class will assess together. Choose something students are familiar with as consumers or employees. Good options:

| Company | Why It Works for This Exercise |
|---|---|
| **Amazon** | Students use it daily; strong in data/compute but has documented ethical failures (hiring tool) |
| **Uber/Lyft** | Relatable; dynamic pricing is visible to students; interesting talent and ethics dimensions |
| **McDonald's** | Students have worked there or visit regularly; drive-through AI, app recommendations, supply chain |
| **Baptist Health South Florida** | Local healthcare system; high-stakes AI applications; strong ethics discussion |

**Recommended for walkthrough:** A company where scores will be uneven across dimensions — strong in some areas, weak in others. This creates richer discussion than a company that's uniformly strong or weak.

---

### Step 2: Score Each Dimension (Discussion-Led)

Walk through each dimension one at a time. For each:
1. Read the key question aloud
2. Ask students to call out a score (1–5) — take 2–3 responses
3. Ask for justification: "Why did you say 3? What evidence supports that?"
4. Push back gently: "You said their data is strong — but how do you know? What kind of data would they need for this AI application?"
5. Land on a consensus score with a 2–3 sentence justification

#### Dimension 1: Data Infrastructure 🗃️

**Discussion prompts:**
- What kind of data does this company collect? (Transactions? Customer behavior? Sensor data? Employee records?)
- Is the data likely to be clean and organized, or messy and scattered?
- Can different parts of the organization access the same data, or is it siloed?
- How long have they been collecting data? Do they have enough historical data for AI?

**What to listen for:** Students often overestimate data readiness. Push them on specifics: "You said they have 'lots of data.' But is it structured? Is it accessible to their data team? Is it updated in real time or quarterly?"

#### Dimension 2: Talent & Skills 👥

**Discussion prompts:**
- Does this company have a data science or AI team? How do you know?
- If they don't have AI specialists, do they have people with adjacent skills (data analysts, IT staff)?
- Are they investing in AI training for existing employees?
- If their top AI person left tomorrow, could they continue their AI initiatives?

**What to listen for:** Students sometimes conflate "they use AI" with "they have AI talent." Many companies buy AI products from vendors — that's different from having internal AI capability.

#### Dimension 3: Computing Resources 💻

**Discussion prompts:**
- Does this company likely run its own servers, use cloud computing, or both?
- What scale of AI workload do they need? (Real-time predictions for millions of users? Batch processing weekly reports?)
- Could a small business version of this company do the same AI work on cheaper hardware?

**What to listen for:** Students may not know the specifics — that's okay. Guide them to think about scale: "If Uber needs to calculate a price for every ride request in real time across every city, what kind of computing power does that take?"

#### Dimension 4: Ethical Preparedness ⚖️

**Discussion prompts:**
- Has this company had any public AI ethics controversies? (Search if needed)
- Do they have a published AI ethics policy or responsible AI principles?
- If their AI system made a biased decision, would they catch it? How quickly?
- Who is responsible for AI fairness at this company — is there a specific person or team?

**What to listen for:** This is often the lowest-scoring dimension. Students may say "they probably check for bias" — push back: "How do you know? Is there a published process? Have they ever disclosed a bias audit?"

#### Dimension 5: Organizational Culture 🏢

**Discussion prompts:**
- Does leadership talk about AI publicly? (Earnings calls, press releases, CEO statements)
- Do front-line employees use AI tools, or is AI only in the back office?
- Is this company known for innovation, or are they more traditional?
- If you worked there and proposed an AI project, would leadership support it?

**What to listen for:** Culture is the hardest dimension to score from the outside. Guide students to look for signals: company blog posts about AI, executive quotes, product launches that feature AI prominently, or — on the negative side — employee reviews mentioning resistance to change.

### Step 4: Identify Gaps & Recommendations

**Ask the class:**
1. Which dimension scored lowest? Is that surprising?
2. What is one specific, actionable step this company could take in the next 6 months to improve their weakest dimension?
3. If you were advising the CEO, what would you tell them to do *first*?

**Instructor note:** Emphasize that the recommendation should be specific and achievable — not "get better data" but "implement automated data quality checks on their transaction database" or "hire a data ethics specialist to conduct quarterly bias audits."

---

### Step 5: Closing Discussion

**Key takeaways to reinforce:**

- The readiness framework applies to ANY organization — from Sofia's restaurant to Amazon
- Most organizations are uneven — strong in some dimensions, weak in others
- Ethical preparedness is almost always the weakest dimension — because it's the newest expectation
- A low total score doesn't mean "don't do AI" — it means "start here, not there"
- The same framework you just used is what you'll apply individually in the Group Lab and Homework

**Bridge to Group Lab:** "Now you'll do this on your own in your groups — with a different company. Each group member will take 1–2 dimensions. You'll score, justify, and identify gaps — just like we did together."

## Completed Example: Marriott International

**AI Use Case:** Dynamic pricing — AI adjusts room rates in real time based on demand, competitor pricing, events, and market conditions.

| Dimension | Score | Justification |
|---|:---:|---|
| Data Infrastructure | 5 | Marriott collects massive amounts of booking data, guest profiles, competitor rates, and market signals across 8,700+ properties worldwide. Data flows in real time and feeds directly into pricing models. |
| Talent & Skills | 5 | Dedicated revenue management and data science teams. Developed an internal "AI Academy" to train staff on AI tools. Partnered with Microsoft and Publicis Sapient on AI initiatives. |
| Computing Resources | 5 | Enterprise-scale cloud infrastructure (Microsoft Azure partnership). Processes pricing decisions across thousands of properties simultaneously in real time. |
| Ethical Preparedness | 3 | Dynamic pricing boosted revenue but devalued loyalty points significantly, frustrating long-time customers. Has had major data breaches (2014–2018, 2020) affecting hundreds of millions of guest records. Published responsible AI principles but track record shows gaps in execution. |
| Organizational Culture | 4 | Leadership actively champions AI (CEO and CTO regularly discuss AI strategy publicly). Revenue teams retrained to work with AI pricing tools. But franchisee adoption varies — not all 8,700 properties are equally AI-integrated. |
| **Total** | **22 / 25** | |

**Biggest gap:** Ethical Preparedness (3/5). Despite strong technical capabilities, Marriott's loyalty point devaluation and data breach history show that ethics and customer trust haven't kept pace with technical deployment.

**Recommended first step:** Appoint a dedicated AI Ethics Officer (or expand the existing team) to conduct impact assessments before new AI features launch — specifically evaluating how pricing changes affect different customer segments (loyalty members vs. new customers, budget travelers vs. business travelers).
