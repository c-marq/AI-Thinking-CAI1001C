# AI-Powered Application Builder — Final Project
**CAI1001C: AI Thinking | 100 Points | Alternative to Final Portfolio**

---

## 📌 Project Overview

In this project, you'll build a simple AI-powered web application that addresses a real-world problem using one of the AI capabilities we've studied this semester — image understanding, language processing, classification, chatbots, or another capability we covered.

This project is the **synthesis** of everything we've learned in AI Thinking. You're not just building an app. You're directing an AI tool to build one for you, making design decisions along the way, and reflecting on what those decisions reveal about how AI works and who it serves.

**Important:** Your app doesn't need to be perfect or fully functional. The goal is to *explore, experiment, and connect what you build to what you've learned.* A rough app with sharp thinking scores higher than a polished app with shallow reflection. This is **AI Thinking**, not AI Building.

---

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By completing this project, you will:

- Apply AI concepts from the course to design and build a working application
- Make deliberate ethical and design choices grounded in semester discussions
- Direct an AI tool to produce code, evaluating and refining its output
- Connect your build decisions explicitly to course chapters, cases, and concepts
- Reflect on what it's like to use AI to build with AI

---

## 🛠️ Which Tool to Use

**Recommended:** Google AI Studio. It's free, has a generous tier for building and testing, and requires no setup beyond a Google account. If you've never built an app before, this is the easiest path.

**Also fine:** If you have experience building apps in other IDEs or tools — VS Code, Cursor, Windsurf, Replit, Claude Code, Bolt, v0, or anything else — use whatever you're comfortable with. The course's goal is to assess your AI thinking, not to make you learn a specific product.

**Whatever you choose, your app must be built with AI assistance** (vibe coding, code generation, agent-directed development). You are not expected to write code by hand.

## 📋 Project Requirements

### Part 1: Build Your AI Application (50 points)

Build an AI-powered application using any AI-assisted tool you're comfortable with (see "Which Tool to Use" above). Your app should:

- Address a real-world problem, not a toy demo
- Have at least **ONE working AI feature** (even if other parts are incomplete)
- Be demonstrable — either live in a browser, in the tool's preview panel, or through a recorded demo and screenshots

**You do not need to deploy your app publicly or pay for any hosting.** If your tool requires billing to deploy, stop there — a working preview or a recorded walkthrough is enough.

**Use the "vibe coding" workflow** — describe what you want in plain English, let the AI generate the code, test it, refine it.

---

### Part 2: Recorded Demonstration (15 points)

Create a short recorded demonstration of your app. Format is flexible:

- A 3–5 minute screen recording (Panopto, Loom, Zoom, QuickTime, etc.)
- A slide deck with screenshots and voiceover (PowerPoint, Canva, etc.)
- Any similar format you're comfortable with

Your demonstration should include:

1. Show your app interface
2. Demonstrate at least ONE feature (working or attempted)
3. Briefly explain:
   - What problem your app solves
   - What's working vs. what isn't
   - One interesting challenge you encountered
   - One course concept that shaped your design

**If your app runs live but can't be deployed publicly**, record your screen while using it in the tool's preview panel or local environment. That counts.

**If your app doesn't fully run**, walk through your code or interface with screenshots and explain what was supposed to happen. An honest demo of broken work is better than a misleading demo of working work.

---

### Part 3: Reflection Document (35 points)

Submit **one consolidated reflection document**, no longer than **1 page** (double-spaced, 12pt font). This is your single written deliverable. It must include all five sections below.

**Section 1 — Project Idea (5 points)**
Briefly describe your app: what does it do, who is it for, what AI capability does it use, and which chapter of the course introduced that capability? What's one ethical consideration you thought about while building? (Reference a specific Reflect & Discuss prompt or case by name — COMPAS, facial recognition bias, language bias, etc.)

**Section 2 — What you built and why (5 points)**
Expand on your app briefly. What real-world problem does it address, and why did you pick this one?

**Section 3 — Ethics in design (10 points)**
This is the heart of the reflection. Describe at least **one ethical decision you made during the build** — something you added, removed, limited, or disclosed because of an ethical consideration. Connect it explicitly to a specific Reflect & Discuss prompt, case, or chapter from the semester by name. A generic "AI has risks" paragraph will not earn full credit here. Specificity is the point.

**Section 4 — What you learned about directing AI (10 points)**
You didn't write the code by hand. You directed an AI tool to write it for you. What did that experience teach you about how AI works, what it's good at, and where it falls short? How is this different from the classifiers and models we built earlier in the semester (Ch 7–9)?

**Section 5 — What you'd change (5 points)**
If you had more time, what would you add, fix, or remove? Frame at least one of these changes as an ethical or design improvement, not just a feature request.

Keep it tight. 1 page is enough if you're specific.

## 🎓 Professor's Note

This project might feel uncomfortable at first — you're building something you've never built before, using a tool that behaves unpredictably, and you're being asked to connect your build decisions to ethical principles we discussed weeks ago. That's the point.

In the real world, AI doesn't show up as a test question. It shows up as a product decision, a design constraint, a judgment call about who gets included and who gets overlooked. This project is the closest you'll get in this course to that kind of decision-making.

I'm not grading you on whether your app works perfectly. I'm grading you on whether you **built something real, made deliberate choices along the way, and thought seriously about what those choices mean.**

Be curious. Be honest about what confused you. And have fun exploring what AI can actually do when you point it at a problem you care about. 🚀
