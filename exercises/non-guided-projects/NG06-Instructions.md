## Overview

Tonight you saw how machine learning works — data goes in, patterns get learned, predictions come out. But that was a classroom example. In this assignment, you'll go find ML **in the real world**, use it yourself, and think critically about what's happening behind the scenes.

**This is not a coding assignment.** You're writing, thinking, and analyzing.

### Step 2: Describe What You Found (200–300 words)

Answer ALL of the following:

**a) What is the tool/app?** What does it do? How did you use it?

**b) What type of machine learning is happening?** (Use what you learned tonight)
- Is it **supervised or unsupervised** learning?
- Is it **regression** (predicting a number) or **classification** (predicting a category)? Or something else?
- If you're not sure, make your best guess and explain your reasoning.

**c) What are the features and label?**
- What **inputs** (features) does the model use? What data is it looking at?
- What **output** (label) is it predicting or generating?

**d) How well did it work?**
- Did it get things right? Where did it struggle?
- Give at least one specific example of a success and one of a failure or limitation.

---

### Step 3: Reflect on It (200–300 words)

This is the most important part. Answer ALL of the following:

**a) What data trained this model?**
- Where do you think the training data came from?
- Do you think the training data represents everyone equally? Who might be overrepresented? Who might be missing?

**b) Who does this tool work well for? Who might it work worse for?**
- Think about language, culture, age, geography, ability, or economic status.
- Be specific — don't just say "some people." Say who and why.

**c) What surprised you, impressed you, or concerned you?**
- Give your honest reaction. There's no right answer.
- Connect at least one observation to something from tonight's class (the presentation, demo, or group lab).

**d) If this tool made a wrong prediction or decision that affected someone's life — a job application, a loan, a medical diagnosis — who should be responsible?**
- The developer? The company? The user? The training data collectors?
- Pick a position and explain it in 2–3 sentences.

## Tips

- **Don't overthink the ML classification.** If you're not sure whether something is supervised or unsupervised, explain what you DO know and why you're making that call. Reasoning matters more than the right label.
- **Be specific in your reflection.** "It might not work for some people" is weak. "It struggled to recognize my voice when I spoke in Spanglish, which suggests the training data was mostly standard English" is strong.
- **Connect back to class.** Reference the scatter plot, the coefficients, the training data discussion, the group lab — anything from tonight. Show me you were paying attention AND thinking.
