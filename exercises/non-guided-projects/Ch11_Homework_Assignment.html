2A. VADER Accuracy (10 points)

In the GP demo, VADER achieved approximately 89% accuracy on the restaurant reviews dataset. Answer both questions:

What does "89% accuracy" mean in this context — what is VADER getting right and wrong? (2-3 sentences)

2B. VADER Failures on Individual Reviews (10 points)

In the GP demo, VADER scored "I can't believe how bad this place has gotten. Used to be great." as POSITIVE (0.79). Explain:

Why did VADER get this wrong? What specific words is VADER reacting to, and what is it failing to understand about the sentence? (3-4 sentences)

2C. The Language Bias Gap (15 points)

In the GP demo, we tested 5 formal/informal review pairs. The formal reviews averaged a VADER score of +0.32 while the informal/Spanglish reviews averaged +0.02 — a gap of +0.30.

Why do formal English reviews consistently score higher than Spanglish/informal reviews, even when they express the same sentiment? Connect your answer to how VADER works (its dictionary, its rules). (3-4 sentences)

The Spanglish review "La comida was fire bro, the service was on point no cap" was classified as NEGATIVE by both VADER and HuggingFace. If even the "smart" model makes the same mistake, what does this tell us about the training data these models learned from? (3-4 sentences)

2D. Real-World Impact (15 points)

Imagine a restaurant chain in Miami uses a sentiment analysis tool (like VADER or HuggingFace) to automatically sort customer reviews. Reviews classified as "negative" go to the manager's urgent inbox. Reviews classified as "positive" go to a thank-you auto-reply.

Based on what you observed in the GP demo and your own testing, describe two specific ways this system could go wrong. For each, explain who would be affected and how. (4-6 sentences total)
